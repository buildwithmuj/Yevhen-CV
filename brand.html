01 · Wordmark

Lowercase name, ultramarine full stop

The mark is the name set in DM Sans Bold with a terminating period in the accent colour. The period is the brand: it says the sentence is finished, the work shipped.

yevhen.

Primary mark

Lowercase, weight 700, letter-spacing −0.02em. Used in the header and anywhere the brand signs small.

Yevhen Harmash.

Formal mark

Full name with the same accent period. Used on the CV and PDF where a document signature is expected.

yevhen.

Never

No letter-spacing on the mark, no uppercase, no recolouring the period. The dot is always the accent, never lime.

02 · Colour

Neutral ground, one loud pair

The palette is neutral-dominant. Two accents carry all the energy: electric ultramarine does the work (actions, links, structure) and acid lime does the punctuation (highlights, status, the ring). Never let them compete in the same moment.

Colour rules

  • Roughly 90% neutral, 8% ultramarine, 2% lime per view.
  • Lime is punctuation: one highlighted phrase, one ring, one badge. Never a text colour on white.
  • Text on lime is always near-black #101010.
  • On dark grounds the accent steps up to #5A48FF; #3D2AFF sinks into black.
  • Semantic red (#FF6B6B) exists only for form validation and is not a brand colour.

03 · Typography

DM Sans, and nothing else

One family across site, CV and PDF: DM Sans (variable, weights 400–700, italic for a single word of emphasis at most). Display sizes track tight; uppercase labels track wide; numbers that line up use tabular figures.

Type rules

  • Weight 500 for display, 600 for card and job titles, 700 only for the wordmark and micro-labels.
  • Grey shading inside headlines (ink → mid → light) creates hierarchy without a second colour.
  • Form inputs never drop below 16px, so iOS Safari does not zoom on focus.
  • Italic exists for one word at a time, as in “Let's talk.”

04 · Signature motifs

The details that say “yevhen.”

Five recurring devices tie every surface together. Each carries meaning; none is pure decoration.

Skills

The lime ring

A hand-drawn ellipse that draws itself around the active thing: the open section, the hovered menu link. Rotated −2°, stroke 2.5, drawn in 0.9s. Hover this card to see it.

a great customer experience

The highlighter

One phrase per statement gets the lime marker. Padding clones across line wraps so the box never breaks ragged.

Yevhen

The cursor badge

A collaborative-cursor chip that signs the hero, drifting on a 5s loop. The author is present in the work.

TS

Floating chips

Rounded-square skill chips drift in hero whitespace, balanced evenly above and below the headline. Emphasis follows the tag system: accent fill for core, lime for AI.

The pulse divider

Sections break on a hairline fading toward a centre dot with a slow radar pulse, alternating ultramarine and lime down the page.

Label dash

The label dash

Uppercase labels open with a 2px accent stroke: the same gesture on the site's eyebrows and the PDF's section labels.

05 · Components

Buttons, tags, surfaces

Buttons

Rectangular accent for the primary action, inverted pill for navigation-weight actions, lime only for the final commit of a form. All lift 1–2px on hover.

The tag system

Four tiers of emphasis, and the tier is information: accent fill marks the core stack, black-and-white marks ways of working, lime marks the AI workflow, outline marks everything supporting.

Dark panels

Forms and the contact block live on #141417 in both themes, with white-alpha hairlines and underline inputs whose focus state is lime.

Project cards

Near-black cards with a brand-tinted bloom from the bottom-right corner, a scrim to keep the text legible, and a tabular sequence number. Radius 1.5rem; panels and pills share the same rounding logic (999px for pills, 1.375–1.75rem for surfaces, 0.5–1rem for small elements).

07 · Voice

Ship, don't boast

Sentence case everywhere except uppercase labels. Active verbs, concrete nouns, no invented metrics, and no em dashes; commas, colons and full stops do the work.

Say
I ship features from first commit to production.
Not
Passionate results-driven developer leveraging synergies.
Say
A product to ship, a team to grow, or a role to fill?

+40% engagement (unless the number is real)
